# This Pushup Challenge Could Finally Make You Put Down Your Phone

YouTuber Laurie Shaw loves a fitness challenge. He's attempted to do CrossFit everyday for 30 days, trained like Chris Hemsworth for 6 weeks, tread water for 24 consecutive hours, and took on the 'Bring Up Sally' pushup challenge, too.

[You Put](https://skin.ketopantrylist.com/blog/1285826508.html)

But his latest fitness challenge has a twist. He's attempting to slash the amount of time he spends on his phone each day by forcing himself to do pushups every time he's on his phone. He's trying this tough discipline for an entire seven days—could you make it a week with that condition attached to using your smartphone?

[Could Finally Make You](https://focus-and-calm.sunsimiaosd.com/)

"Last week I set up my screen time on my iPhone, where it tells me exactly how many hours I spend each day looking at my phone," he says. "Ah man, I was embarrassed. It's ridiculous. But it made me think, how can I reduce the number of hours I stare at my phone?"

Shaw found out that he spends an average of 5 hours and 30 minutes each day on his phone. To combat this time suck, he created a new challenge for himself. Every time he touches his phone, he has to do 10 pushups. And if he picks up his phone without doing the 10 pushups, he has to do 20 pushups. The only exception is if someone calls him and he absolutely needs to respond. His goal is to cut his daily phone time down to three hours—and get super ripped.

"I feel like this is a win-win. Either I'm going to reduce the number of hours (I'm on my phone), or I'm going to get super fit in the process," he says.

He ends up having to rep out pushups all over the place—on rocks near the ocean, the supermarket, the street, and more. After struggling to stay off his device, by the end of Day 2, he was actively trying not to pick up his phone.

[Finally Make You Put](https://raazpeptide.com/category/x45e93f)

"It's so addicting. But I've definitely checked it less today than I have in years," he says. "I'm touching my phone less, but when I do, it's more intentional."

The challenge worked, and at the end of Day 7, he's averaged between 1 to 3 hours of screen time.

"It's just good to know I'm being more conscious of when I pick up my phone," he says.
