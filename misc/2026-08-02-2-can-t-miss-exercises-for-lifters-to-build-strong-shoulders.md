# 2 Can't-Miss Exercises for Lifters to Build Strong Shoulders

Dr. Aaron Horschig, DPT, of Squat University, is always eager to help lifters fix their mobility and stability issues. He's sharing his two favorite shoulder exercises to help weightlifters keep their shoulders strong and healthy. (In case you missed it, he previously shared the three shoulder warmups you should never skip.)

"As weightlifters, powerlifters and CrossFitters, we're always doing a lot of strength work with both of our arms in contact with the barbell. But often that can hide and expose problems in mobility and stability that hamper technique and set us up for eventual injury," says Horschig.

He shares his two favorite exercises to help you build strong, healthy shoulders and keep you resilient to injury. And all you need is a small kettlebell or dumbbell. He suggests doing 3 to 5 reps of each exercise on each arm.

Exercise 1: The Kettlebell Windmill

Do this move prior to your workout.

"This is going to help produce purposeful stability and mobility in movements that are going to carry over to a jerk, snatch, press or any movement overhead," says Horschig. "It's going to focus on the proper mechanics of the shoulder blade and prime proper shoulder and upper body mobility."

To do the windmill, get on your knees, with one leg in front of the other. Grab the kettlebell in one hand and brace your core. Push the kettlebell up above your head, with your arm ending up by your ear. Keep your core braced, and don't arch your low back. With the kettlebell still above your head, tip to the side and plant your opposite palm on the ground, forming a perfect line from the floor to the kettlebell. Squeeze your shoulder blade to your spine, gripping the kettlebell as much as possible, holing for a couple seconds, then rotating again, removing your hand from the floor and setting your shoulder back in a locked out position.

Exercise 2: The Turkish Kettlebell Getup

This move is a progression from the windmill, and Horschig suggests doing this more difficult maneuver after your workout.

Start on the ground, and keep your left leg out straight, your right knee tucked, holding the kettlebell with the right arm. Lay down on the floor, and push the kettlebell straight up.

"A cue I like to use is pretend you have a cup of you favorite drink in your hand. Do not let anything from that cup spill out during this entire movement, always having your fits toward directly towards the sky and core braced," says Horschig.
