# Try These 2 Exercises to Build Up Bigger Back Muscles

Australian trainer Eugene Teo wants to help you to grow big muscles. He's shared his two favorite moves that have helped him build size and strength in his quads, along with his top exercises that have taken his biceps growth to the next level. Now, he's sharing his two go-to exercises to get serious back gains.

In case you're wondering no: neither of these exercises are a pullup.

Exercise 1: Lat Pulldown

[Up Bigger](https://therapy.bairibu.com/blog/8485015864.html)

Teo loves a lat pulldown, but has a different technique to his than what you may do traditionally. And that's because he takes major issue with the traditional lat pulldown for two reason.

Reason 1: The setup doesn't provide much stability."The only thing holding you in position in a typical lat pulldown is your legs being anchored into position," says Teo. "It's sufficient, but with your torso being suspended in mid-air like this, you have to expend a lot of effort and energy to keep yourself in a good position to work your back muscles without any momentum."

He says that this can shift the work away from your lats and onto different muscles, like your shoulders, biceps, and neck.

Reason 2: Alignment isn't ideal."The way most people do a lat pulldown isn't naturally a good position for the lats to be contracting, and instead put the muscles of the upper back into a much better position overall," says Teo. "The lat fibers don't get taken through a complete range of motion."

To overcome these issues, he uses an incline bench anchored up behind a lat pulldown machine.

"It provides you with something to brace your body up against," says Teo. "And the bench position puts the lat muscles into near perfect alignment against the line of the cable, which means the lats are working very hard through the entire range of motion."

To do it properly, he has a couple tips.

"Make sure you're driving the elbows down in front of your torso towards your thighs, and to avoid arching through your back and pulling the elbows behind your torso and squeezing your shoulder blades together. Because as soon as you do these things, you'll move tension away from the lats and on to muscles of the upper back," says Teo.

If you don't have access to a cable machine or a bench, you can try a pulldown with a resistance band from a kneeling position to solve some of these problems.
